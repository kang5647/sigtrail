THE INVESTMENT READ

Useful infrastructure.
Demand breadth remains unproven.

NEAR accounts request derived-key signatures through a shared MPC service. Applications can use this infrastructure to authorize actions across chains.

Concentrated account activity does not establish broad application adoption or material NEAR token capture.

Signing can authorize arbitrary payloads. It does not necessarily represent a cross-chain transfer, a successful signature, or destination-chain settlement.

What was counted, and what was excluded?

One request = one distinct retained receipt containing a FunctionCall whose exact method is sign, filtered to v1.signer during collection. MPC respond, internal completion and other methods are excluded. A receipt containing multiple sign actions still counts once.

The query selects parent transactions included in 11 November UTC, blocks 223,988,378–224,135,609. Descendant receipts may fall outside that interval. FastNear index completeness has not been independently established against a full node.

Correction: older archives retain receipt creation times, not execution times. One direct receipt was created after midnight; the number executed outside the day is unknown. Counts and concentration are unaffected.

What would strengthen or weaken the investment thesis?

Strengthen

Repeat matched complete-day cohorts; document unrelated application operators; show sustained demand across those operators; link signatures to destination settlements; measure the entire NEAR execution path and compare attributed burn with contemporaneous issuance.

Weaken

Persistently concentrated infrastructure activity, apparent new accounts belonging to existing operators, requests failing to settle, or low attributable economic activity despite higher request counts.

Utility depends on MPC threshold security and availability. Outbound signing alone does not verify inbound chain state. This research does not establish superiority over competing signing systems or a token price implication.
