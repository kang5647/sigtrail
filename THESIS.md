# Thesis: demand quality is the missing Chain Signatures metric

**Question.** Does NEAR's Chain Signatures primitive become a platform used by a diverse set of applications, or does apparent activity mainly reflect callbacks and a few infrastructure integrations?

**Why NEAR matters.** A NEAR account or contract can request an MPC signature for transactions on other chains from `v1.signer`, removing the need for each app to operate separate signing infrastructure. That shared primitive is the potential network advantage. The investment case depends on independent applications adopting it repeatedly and on some durable route from usage to NEAR-token value. Usage alone is insufficient.

## The investment decision

The long-term bull case is conditional: a shared NEAR signing service could become useful infrastructure across many independent applications, and growing use could burn more NEAR through execution fees. The current evidence clears the narrow **utility** and **per-receipt supply-burn mechanism** tests. It does not yet clear the **diversified demand** or **economic materiality** tests. This is a decision to withhold a strong positive Chain Signatures investment claim, not a prediction that adoption will fail.

| Test | Evidence in this build | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the primitive do useful cross-chain work? | Direct mainnet `sign` receipts are observable; one indexed NEAR-to-Bitcoin association ends in a confirmed Bitcoin transaction. | Supported as a capability and one case, not as a settlement-rate estimate. |
| Are independent applications using it broadly? | In twelve spaced windows across 11 November, the top three initiating accounts made 1,219 of 1,482 requests (82.3%). | Broad independent demand is not established by these samples. Accounts are not necessarily separate businesses. |
| Does this use materially affect NEAR supply? | Eighty direct `sign` receipt outcomes averaged 0.000418941 NEAR in reported execution charge. The sampled mainnet block used protocol v88, which removes burnt gas from supply. | A direct supply-burn route exists for this stage; its scale against issuance and the rest of the workflow remain unmeasured. Burn is not a price or holder-income claim. |

The three tests are deliberately separate. The dashboard's editable model is a conditional calculation, not evidence that any assumed volume or independent-app share is real. NEAR Intents buybacks below are an ecosystem comparison, not proceeds from the `v1.signer` requests counted here.

## Evidence refreshed 13 November 2026 UTC

The mainnet audit was rebuilt from [FastNear indexed transaction traces](https://docs.fastnear.com/tx). The 25-point raw-activity series samples one 1,000-parent-transaction-block interval every million block heights. Seven of those windows are fully hydrated to classify methods, at fixed four-million-block intervals. Each window spans around 9–10 minutes. The original three audited windows were fixed before the first method classification; the four extra windows were chosen at equal intervals before classifying their methods. The samples are neither random nor exhaustive. Signer receipts may execute after their parent transaction's block range.

The **11 November top three**—`omni.bridge.near` (43), `fast-auth.near` (22), and `btc-connector.bridge.near` (7)—accounted for **72 of 80 direct requests, or 90%**. This is the sharpest result. The top-three share in the seven windows ranged from **74.8% to 90.0%**. `fast-auth.near` and bridge accounts recur; `tx-bench.near` appears among the leaders in two windows, which is not evidence of independent production application demand. These samples show repeated concentration, but their short duration cannot establish a network-wide adoption trend or measure unique applications. An account is not necessarily a distinct business.

The 11 November window also contained 85 MPC `respond` receipts, 80 internal signature completions, and eight other calls. Those are real contract work, but not additional direct signature requests. The data neither proves broad adoption nor proves decline; these short windows do not support a network-wide growth estimate.

### One-day concentration stress test

We also fixed twelve 1,000-block windows starting at block 224,000,000 and separated by 12,000 block heights, before classifying the additional eleven. They run from **01:50 to 23:34 UTC on 11 November**, at roughly two-hour intervals. The first is the 11 November historical window above, not an extra independent observation. The twelve windows contain **1,482 direct `sign` receipts** from **18 initiating accounts**. Pooling and deduplicating those receipts, `omni.bridge.near` (686), `fast-auth.near` (287), and `btc-connector.bridge.near` (246) made **1,219 of 1,482 requests, or 82.3%**. Each window's own top-three share ranged from **78.3% to 90.6%**.

This is stronger evidence that concentration persists across the sampled day; it is still **spaced sampling, not a full-day census**. The selected windows cover about nine minutes every two hours. They do not show what happened between windows, and the account labels do not establish independent business ownership. The exact ranges, receipts, and caller labels are in the pipeline and CSV.

An additional, **unpooled offset check** queried blocks **224,006,000–224,006,999**, halfway between the first two spaced windows. It found **96** direct `sign` receipts from **11** initiating accounts, with **78 of 96 (81.25%)** from the top three. Re-run it with `npm run spot-check -- 224006000`. This is one selected offset, not a random holdout or statistical proof that every unobserved interval looks the same; it is not added to the 1,482-request day sample.

The wider series contains 25 fixed 1,000-block raw-activity probes from May to November. Matching transaction counts range from **97 to 534 per window**. They add longitudinal context but cannot be relabeled as `sign` requests because only seven windows are method-audited. The first and last observations (418 and 170) are not a network-wide growth rate.

One separate **illustrative settlement case** came from a [NearBlocks multichain-signature index record](https://api.nearblocks.io/v3/multichain/signatures?chain=BITCOIN&txn=75a2455fdab91a57c89980f39df4ae50b8d094b28d6e74a87e46468f1148edd7): [this NEAR transaction](https://pikespeak.ai/transaction-viewer/FrBQdrUigUK1JYaXyVqA7uJH4PvLYJVCHD1iPUn1UrFG) contains a `v1.signer.sign` receipt whose predecessor is `btc-connector.bridge.near`; the associated [Bitcoin transaction](https://mempool.space/tx/75a2455fdab91a57c89980f39df4ae50b8d094b28d6e74a87e46468f1148edd7) is confirmed in block **975,572**. The refresh now verifies that the index record names those exact NEAR transaction, receipt, and Bitcoin transaction IDs. FastNear confirms the NEAR method, while Mempool.space and Blockstream agree on Bitcoin confirmation. The cross-chain association is indexed, not independently cryptographically reconstructed from signature bytes. One case does not establish a network settlement rate.

### Separate token-value test

The aligned 30-day period **14 October–12 November 2026** shows approximately **$5.81 million** in NEAR Intents gross fees and **$1.84 million** in DeFiLlama's holder-revenue/buyback measure, or **31.6%** of gross fees. Gross fees include integrator affiliate fees. This is a separate Intents metric, **not** revenue caused by `v1.signer`. NEAR's own [revenue dashboard](https://revenue.near.org/) covers Intents capture more comprehensively; Capture Test's original contribution is the method-level signer audit and attribution. Chain Signatures has a distinct native fee-burn route below, whose materiality remains to be tested.

The native execution footprint can be measured more directly, but only narrowly. In the 11 November historical window, FastNear's execution outcomes for the **80 direct `sign` receipts** reported **0.033515 NEAR** in `tokens_burnt`, averaging **0.000418941 NEAR per receipt**. [The 80 outcome rows](public/data/fee-audit.csv) are downloadable. An archived mainnet `EXPERIMENTAL_protocol_config` query at block **224,000,000** returned protocol version **88** and `burnt_gas_reward = [0, 1]`; [nearcore's gas documentation](https://github.com/near/nearcore/blob/master/docs/architecture/how/gas.md) states that from version 87 burnt execution gas is removed from total supply with no contract gas rebate. Thus the measured **0.033515 NEAR is a protocol supply burn for this receipt stage**, not just an unknown fee allocation. Run `npm run protocol-check` to re-query archived protocol configuration at blocks 200,000,000, 220,000,000, and 224,000,000; the script checks the historical rebate change before accepting the burn interpretation.

At the same observed per-receipt charge and protocol rule, **10,000 / 100,000 / 1,000,000 direct requests per day** would imply **4.19 / 41.89 / 418.94 NEAR per day** of hypothetical `sign`-stage supply burn. These are sensitivities, not observed demand, a full-workflow burn estimate, net deflation, holder income, or a price forecast. The one-day audit observed 1,482 requests in spaced windows, not a full-day total; none of the three scenario volumes is asserted as actual traffic. The fee study excludes the parent transaction, MPC response, internal completion, other contracts, and destination-chain fees. We have not compared this partial burn figure with verified issuance over the same period. The small measured per-receipt charge reinforces why request counts alone are an incomplete investment thesis.

The version-88 supply-burn inference applies to the **November fee sample only**. Earlier historical windows ran under older protocol versions with a contract gas rebate; this project has not measured their receipt-level fees or net burn. The seven-window demand comparison must not be converted into a seven-window burn trend.

The dashboard lets a reader change three inputs: assumed direct requests per day, assumed share initiated by **independent applications**, and an assumed fee multiple relative to the observed mean (default **1×**). It computes `0.000418941 NEAR × requests/day × independent-app share × fee multiple` for hypothetical independent-app `sign`-stage burn per day, then multiplies by 365 for an annual sensitivity. **Only the per-receipt charge is measured here.** The default 25% independent-app share is an input, not an estimate: the 18 caller accounts have not been mapped to independently owned businesses, and the observed 17.7% share outside the top three cannot be substituted for it. Neither output is a valuation, a traffic forecast, or an estimate of protocol-wide net burn.

## Current judgment

**Conditional, with repeated concentration in the sampled windows.** Chain Signatures is a real NEAR primitive; the seven historical windows show direct requests from 8–12 initiating accounts each, with top-three concentration of 74.8–90.0%. The denser day audit puts the pooled top three at 82.3% of 1,482 sampled requests. The execution outcomes and protocol configuration establish a narrow supply-burn mechanism, but the evidence is insufficient to claim a broad independent application ecosystem or material net token impact. Intents buybacks support a different, separately measured economic claim.

## What would change the judgment

**Proposed adoption decision rule:** Fix the same twelve-window cadence before each of four future weekly observation days. A stronger platform thesis would require at least **1,000 direct requests per sampled day**, a top-three caller share below **60% in at least three of four weeks**, and at least **ten independently controlled, non-test application teams** with ten or more requests each in those qualifying weeks. Linked accounts under one owner count as one team. The 1,000-request floor keeps the comparison near the present 1,482-request sample; the 60% hurdle demands substantial diversification from the observed 82.3%, rather than a trivial change. These are decision rules for future evidence, not statistical confidence intervals.

**Proposed economic decision rule:** Trace the complete NEAR execution path for a fixed, documented cohort of direct requests, then measure the **30-day gross Chain Signatures workflow burn attributable to independently identified applications** against **NEAR minted in the same 30 days**. A ratio above **1%** for four consecutive weekly observations would make token impact worth further analysis; below **0.1%** would weaken a material token-value claim even if request counts rise. These thresholds are research hurdles, not a claim that burn equals holder income or price appreciation. This repository has measured only the direct `sign` receipt stage, so it cannot yet evaluate that rule.

**Weaken or falsify adoption:** If the top-three share stays above **80% in at least three of four** future fixed weekly samples, or fewer than **five independently controlled application teams** remain after grouping accounts and excluding test infrastructure, the broad-platform claim is weaker. A rise in raw `v1.signer` account transactions alone is not supporting evidence. Destination-chain outcomes should be traced for a preselected cohort, with intended-but-unbroadcast signatures separated from true settlement failures.

The thresholds above are forward-looking, deliberately demanding research choices. They were written after the current sample was observed and must not be treated as preregistered evidence or significance tests. Future windows, ownership classifications, and cohort selection need to be fixed and archived before those data are inspected.

## Reproduction and limits

Run `npm run refresh` from this repository to update the dashboard while reusing the committed fixed-window receipt audit. For a fresh source-to-result reproduction, run **`npm run refresh -- --full-audit`**; it re-downloads every fixed-window transaction trace and may be delayed by FastNear's public rate limits. The script uses fixed ranges in `scripts/refresh.mjs`, paginates all matching FastNear account transactions for the seven historical and twelve day windows, hydrates their complete traces in batches of 20, classifies the receipts in `src/analysis.mjs`, and stores the filtered receipt IDs, transaction hashes, methods, callers, and timestamps in `public/data/snapshot.json` and `public/data/receipt-audit.csv`. The overlapping window appears once in the CSV. It separately re-fetches all 80 direct `sign` execution outcomes for `public/data/fee-audit.csv`. A one-page count query builds each of the 25 raw-activity points. The same script aligns DeFiLlama feeds on complete UTC dates. The figures in this document are the dated snapshot above; a later refresh changes the dashboard data but does not silently rewrite this interpretation.

FastNear is an indexed service, not an independent archival NEAR node. An API outage or policy change may prevent a fresh rebuild, so the snapshot is committed for audit. The data excludes calls that never reached `v1.signer`, and a direct request can fail or never result in an external transaction. The Bitcoin case depends on NearBlocks for the association and is independently checked only for the two chain records. No price target or probability of winning is inferred.
