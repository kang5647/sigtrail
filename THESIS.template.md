# Capture Test: demand quality is the missing Chain Signatures metric

**Verdict.** NEAR Chain Signatures are demonstrably used, but these receipts do not establish broad demand from independently operated applications. At the observed direct `sign`-receipt burn, plausible and even aggressive adoption sensitivities remain small beside contemporaneous gross NEAR issuance; material token capture is not yet established.

The strongest counterevidence sits beside the utility claim: in the latest fixed window, the top three initiating accounts made **{{LATEST_TOP_THREE_COUNT}}/{{LATEST_DIRECT}} direct requests ({{LATEST_TOP_THREE_PCT}})**; across twelve spaced windows on the same day, the pooled top three made **{{DAY_TOP_THREE_COUNT}}/{{DAY_DIRECT}} ({{DAY_TOP_THREE_PCT}})**. These are accounts, **not unique users or proven independent companies**. The 12 windows are selected, not random, and are not a complete-day census.

## What the receipts establish

A NEAR account can ask `v1.signer` to produce a signature for another chain. This shared signing primitive is the potential platform advantage over each application operating its own signing infrastructure. Capture Test separates transactions merely touching `v1.signer` from receipts whose `receiver_id` is `v1.signer` and whose method is exactly `sign`; MPC callbacks and internal completion work do not count as new requests. [The committed receipt rows](public/data/receipt-audit.csv) include receipt IDs, methods, parent transaction hashes, caller accounts, and timestamps. The dashboard opens an exact receipt and its public parent transaction.

The latest historical window has **{{LATEST_TOUCHING}} signer-touching transactions** but only **{{LATEST_DIRECT}} direct `sign` receipts**. Its leading callers were {{LATEST_CALLERS}}. Across the wider same-day sample, there were **{{DAY_DIRECT}} direct receipts from {{DAY_ACCOUNTS}} distinct initiating account IDs**; the largest alone made **{{DAY_TOP_ONE_COUNT}}/{{DAY_DIRECT}} ({{DAY_TOP_ONE_PCT}})**. Account IDs are not people, companies, or independent applications.

## What usage could mean for the token

**Only the direct `sign` receipt stage is measured.** Its {{FEE_RECEIPTS}} execution outcomes sum to **{{FEE_TOTAL}} NEAR** in `tokens_burnt`, averaging **{{FEE_AVERAGE}} NEAR per receipt** at the sampled November protocol v88. At v88, burnt gas leaves supply; the archived protocol query reports `burnt_gas_reward = [0,1]`. The fee audit excludes parent transactions, MPC responses, cleanup, other contracts, and destination-chain fees. It is **distinct from NEAR Intents fees and buybacks**. Nothing here forecasts token price, total workflow burn, or net supply change.

To put the stage charge in scale, we summed gross validator and treasury rewards reported by archival NEAR `validators` RPC at every epoch transition in the fixed **{{ISSUANCE_PERIOD}}** UTC interval containing the fee sample. The {{ISSUANCE_EPOCH_COUNT}} contiguous epoch rewards total **{{ISSUANCE_TOTAL}} NEAR** minted in seven days, or **{{ISSUANCE_DAILY}} NEAR/day** when normalized by seven. The annual comparison, **{{ISSUANCE_YEARLY}} NEAR**, is 365 times that observed daily rate, **not a claim that future issuance is constant**. This is gross issuance before network-wide fee burn, not net supply growth. Epoch rewards are recorded at transition, so a seven-day window avoids mistaking a quiet transition day for zero structural issuance. The individual epoch IDs, block hashes, timestamps, account rewards, source RPC, and fetch time are in [snapshot.json](public/data/snapshot.json); `npm run issuance-check` re-queries the archived source. The [NEAR economics specification](https://github.com/near/nearcore/blob/master/docs/Economics/Economics.md) describes validator rewards.

DeFiLlama's gross fees include integrator affiliate fees, and its holders-revenue series tracks NEAR bought back, **not NEAR burned by `v1.signer.sign`**. Intents data provide ecosystem context, not evidence that Chain Signatures requests generated those fees.

## What would change the verdict

**Strengthen the application thesis:** Predeclare full-day receipt censuses on multiple future dates; identify operators with documentation rather than account-name inference; show at least ten unrelated, non-test application teams with repeat direct requests; and see the top-three account share fall below 60% in at least three of four comparable weekly audits while sampled direct demand stays above 1,000. These are research hurdles chosen after this sample, not preregistered statistical tests.

**Strengthen the token thesis:** For a preselected 30-day cohort, trace the complete NEAR execution path per direct request and compare its gross burn from independently identified applications with actual NEAR issuance over the same 30 days. A sustained ratio above 1% would justify further token-value analysis. It still would not imply a price response. Separately measure how many signed requests actually settle on destination chains; one Bitcoin case cannot answer that.

**Falsify or weaken it:** Persistent top-three concentration above 80% across three of four future weekly audits, fewer than five independently controlled application operators after documented grouping, or full-workflow burn below 0.1% of contemporaneous issuance despite rising requests would weaken the platform or material-capture case. Raw signer-touching transaction counts alone do not reverse that judgment.

## Reproduce and audit limits

Run `npm test && npm run rebuild` offline. The rebuild reads committed receipts and epoch rewards, deduplicates IDs, reclassifies methods and callers, recalculates fee sums, concentration, issuance, scenarios, thresholds, dashboard files, and this document. It does not need an API key or network access. `npm run refresh` separately fetches live FastNear, DeFiLlama, and cross-chain sources; `npm run issuance-check` independently re-queries archival epoch rewards. Refresh may be throttled and should never be treated as fresh if it fails. The committed snapshot was fetched on **{{FETCH_DATE}}**; its SHA-256 after rebuild is **`{{SNAPSHOT_SHA}}`**. Source URLs, ranges, timestamps, classifications, and row-level CSV are committed.

FastNear is an index, not an independently operated archival NEAR node. Parent-transaction block selection can include receipts executed after the range. The selected historical and same-day windows are not random, representative monthly estimates, or a complete-day census. A direct `sign` receipt can fail to produce an external settlement. The scenario burn applies the November v88 rule only to hypothetical future calls; older historical windows used different gas-rebate rules and are **not** a historical burn series. The most important missing measurements are complete-day direct-request demand, verified operator independence, full-workflow burn, and destination-chain settlement rates.
